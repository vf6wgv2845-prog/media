# What drives shares (DM sends), comments, saves and follows on short-form video (Gen Z, Reels/TikTok, 2025-2026)

Context: small account, animated Reels story series. Reels already hold attention (skip rate ~32-36%), but share rate is 0.1-0.3%, comment rate is 0-0.4%, and 65 profile visits led to 0 follows.

## 1. Why people share short videos (psychology and platform data)

### Takeaway
On Instagram, DM sends are the main signal for reaching non-followers. Mosseri calls "sends per reach" one of the most important ranking signals, and says sends matter more than likes for unconnected reach. The research points the same way. People share content that is high-arousal (funny, awe-inspiring, anger-inducing), positive, useful, or that makes them look in the know (social currency). Gen Z sends videos and memes to show care for someone, a "love language". Content that makes a viewer think of one specific person gets sent.

### Cited Findings
- Adam Mosseri (Instagram head): "One of the most important signals we use in ranking is sends per reach." He defines it as the share of people who saw a post and then sent it by DM. He also says "don't force it as a creator." July 2024. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389/)
- Mosseri, Jan 2025: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends... Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." He tells creators to track average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Comments were not among the top three. — [Social Media Today, Jan 22 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- Meta "Generation Zeitgeist" research with BAMM, 18-24 year olds in the US, UK, Brazil, Germany and Japan, Apr 2025:
  - 67% of Gen Z Facebook/Instagram users have shared video content with friends and family in-app. Sharing on Instagram is higher than on Facebook.
  - Meta describes sending videos and memes as a Gen Z "love language", a way to show care. Gen Z shares "helpful and joyful" content.
  - Meta calls short video a "qualitative search engine".
  - 81% use Instagram for self-development.
  - [Social Media Today, Apr 13 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-research-gen-z-social-media-engagement/745214/)
- Jonah Berger's STEPPS framework:
  - Social Currency: sharing makes you look smart or in the know. Knowledge only works as currency if not everyone has it.
  - Triggers: "top of mind, tip of tongue". Recurring cues prompt sharing.
  - Emotion: "Activating emotions, whether they're positive or negative, drive us to share". Humor and anger are both arousing.
  - Public, Practical Value and Stories make up the rest.
  - [Penn Today Q&A](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/2013-04-18/interviews/qa-jonah-berger)
- Berger and Milkman (J. Marketing Research 2012), ~7,000 NYT articles plus lab studies:
  - Positive content was shared more than negative content.
  - High-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) increased sharing. Sadness, a deactivating emotion, reduced sharing.
  - [Scientific American summary](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-secret-to-online-success-what-makes-content-go-viral/); [paper PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf)
- Socialinsider (21.3M posts, Oct 2025-Mar 2026) on why Reels get shared:
  - People share Reels on instinct when a video is funny, emotionally moving, or "says what they were already thinking".
  - Niche, personally relevant content makes viewers think of a specific person to send it to.
  - A memorable ending raises forwarding.
  - [Socialinsider Instagram Engagement Report, May 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram-engagement-report)
- LSE / Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans (2026), 24 interviews, ages 13-26:
  - Gen Z values "brainrot" for its deliberate absurdity, as escape, as resistance to the attention economy, and as "anti-gratification".
  - It works as "generational in-group formation", a shared cultural marker.
  - [Götzfried & Heitmayer 2026](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/137185)

### Inferences
- The account's attention is fine. The weak point is that viewers have no reason to send. Write each episode around one "send trigger":
  - a recognizable situation ("this is literally you / us")
  - a character archetype viewers map onto a friend
  - a laugh-out-loud or awe payoff
  - an in-joke or lore callback that only fans or friends get
- Avoid endings that resolve on sadness. Unresolved, surprising or funny endings are more arousing and more forwardable.
- A "memorable ending" (Socialinsider) suits story series well. Put the punchline or twist in the final 1-2 seconds so the urge to send comes when the video finishes.
- Because sends drive reach to non-followers, a higher send rate is also the main way to get new profile visitors.

### Gaps
- I found no public Meta study with numbers on the motives behind DM sending (e.g. % "relatable" vs "funny"). Meta's Gen Z data is high-level.
- I found no 2025-2026 peer-reviewed study specifically on short-video DM sharing among 13-17 year olds.

## 2. Driving comments without being penalized as engagement bait

### Takeaway
Instagram won't recommend content that "explicitly asks for engagement through shares, comments, tags" (e.g. "comment a specific word, number, or emoji"). It calls open-ended questions and CTAs "fair game". Specific questions that start real discussion work best ("Which would you pick?"), as do opinions people want to push back on and relatable frustrations. Comments are not among Mosseri's top three ranking signals, so treat comments as community-building, not the main lever for reach.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram creator guidance (deleted video, ~late May 2024), quoted by Social Media Today:
  - "If we believe your content explicitly asks for engagement through shares, comments, tags, or other actions" Instagram "won't recommend it."
  - The example given was "asking people to comment with a specific word, number, or emoji."
  - "open-ended questions and call to actions, they're both fair game." Instagram encourages content that "inspires people to engage in real discussion."
  - Social Media Today reads the penalty as applying to recommendations and non-followers.
  - [Social Media Today, Jun 2 2024](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-says-using-certain-ctas-can-impact-post-reach/717732/)
- Meta lists engagement bait among content whose distribution it reduces. — [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/lowering-distribution-of-problematic-content/); [Instagram recommendation guidelines](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality)
- Socialinsider (May 2026):
  - Reels get the highest comment rate of the three formats because they feel like direct contact that invites a response.
  - Caption hooks that work: a controversial opinion, a direct question, or a relatable frustration.
  - Specific CTAs ("Which of these would you try first?") get more replies than generic ones.
  - [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram-engagement-report)
- Mosseri's Jan 2025 list of top signals leaves out comments. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- TikTok lets creators reply to comments with a new video. Brands and creators use comment threads as engagement venues. I found no hard 2025-2026 data quantifying the lift. — [Hootsuite on TikTok comments](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-comments/); [Modern Retail, Apr 2026](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/why-brands-cant-stop-acting-like-reply-guys-and-jumping-into-viral-comment-threads-on-social-media/)

### Inferences (tactics for an animated story series)
- Build comment prompts into the story itself, not the caption:
  - an A/B choice the character faces ("what should she do next?" lets the audience steer the next episode)
  - a "hidden detail" planted in the frame
  - a debatable character decision
  - an unresolved cliffhanger
- In the caption or pinned comment, ask a specific, opinion-based question. Never "comment X".
- Pin a comment that seeds the debate or confirms the easter egg exists. Reply quickly to early commenters to start threads.
- On TikTok, answer comments with video replies. These double as new episodes.
- Pick topics that are controversial but safe: low-stakes takes (food, school or work quirks, which friend is which archetype). These invite pushback without brand-safety risk.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable public data on the effect of creator reply speed or pinned comments on reach for 2025-2026. Claims online are anecdotal.

## 3. Converting profile visits into follows

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous public dataset on profile-visit-to-follow conversion or on which profile elements raise it. Most "case studies" are vendor blogs with no data. Instagram added post-level "which posts led to the most new followers" insights in Aug 2025, so the account can measure which episodes convert. 65 visits and 0 follows suggests the profile doesn't show the visitor that a series exists, what it is, and where to start.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram began rolling out Insights updates showing "which posts have led to the highest number of new followers" (Aug 5 2025). — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-performance-metrics-audience-growth-insights-9627177)
- Profile-optimization guides were found but are vendor content without verifiable data. — [CreatorFlow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-profile-optimization/); [Boss Wallah](https://bosswallah.com/blog/instagram/instagram-profile-conversion-rate-7-ways-to-turn-profile-visits-into-real-business-results/)

### Inferences (reasoned, not data-backed)
- A visitor decides in about 2 seconds whether more of what they just watched exists.
- Pin 3 posts:
  1. "Episode 1 / Start here"
  2. the best-performing episode
  3. a character intro or "meet the cast"
- Use consistent, numbered series covers ("Ep 4") so the grid reads as a show.
- Put the series concept in the name field and the bio first line, e.g. "[Series name] | animated story about X". Add a reason to follow: posting cadence, "new ep every Tue/Fri", or what comes next.
- End each Reel with an open loop ("Part 5: she finds out...") so following is the way to get the payoff.
- Use the Aug 2025 "follows by post" insight to find which episodes convert, then make more like them.
- If the account is very new, Trial Reels (shown to non-followers) can test hooks without hurting the grid. (Feature knowledge only, not sourced here.)

### Gaps
- No public benchmark for profile-visit-to-follow rate was found. Third-party sites quoting a "typical" % are unverified.
- No controlled data on bios, highlights or "start here" posts.

## 4. Gen Z content preferences 2025-2026 (humor, lore, AI attitudes)

### Takeaway
Gen Z uses video-first apps for entertainment more than for social connection. They like absurd, ironic and "brainrot" humor as in-group culture, but late 2025 showed fatigue with AI-driven, low-effort meme churn (the "Great Meme Reset"). Most of them dislike AI-generated content and want it labeled. For an animated series, visibly human-made craft matters.

### Cited Findings
- BeReal survey, Nov 2025, 2,555 US respondents aged 13-28:
  - 47% dislike AI-generated content and prefer human-made. Only 5% find it interesting or creative. 22% don't mind it if it's labeled.
  - About half say AI harms their social media experience. Only 4% say it improves it.
  - 75% want platforms to label AI content.
  - 34% say it is extremely important that social stays "100% human".
  - [Net Influencer, Dec 5 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/half-of-gen-z-says-ai-harms-social-media-experience-survey-finds/)
- YPulse (Nov 2025): TikTok users describe a "meme drought" they blame on AI-driven brainrot. They propose a "Great Meme Reset" for 2026, nostalgic for the organic memes of 2016, so that virality has to be "earned". No numbers given. — [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2025/11/12/gen-z-is-planning-to-wipe-the-meme-slate-clean-in-2026/)
- YPulse (May 2026): Gen Z turns to Instagram and TikTok for entertainment rather than social connection. Snapchat is their friend-connection app. — [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/article/2026/05/13/the-real-reason-gen-z-is-using-these-social-platforms/)
- Brainrot and absurdist content serves escape, aesthetic enjoyment and in-group identity (2026 qualitative study). — [LSE](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/137185)
- Italian Brainrot (AI-generated absurd characters) was a major 2025 Gen Z trend. — [Fortune, Sep 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/09/07/what-is-italian-brain-rot-gen-z-social-media-tiktok/)

### Inferences
- Recurring characters with lore (running gags, catchphrases, callbacks) give Gen Z in-group currency to share. This is "social currency" in the sense of knowing the reference.
- If AI tools are used, keep them out of sight in the result and avoid the generic AI look. Disclose honestly where required. A human-craft angle (behind-the-scenes, sketches) can be a differentiator.
- Absurd humor works best inside a consistent world. Random absurdity is commoditized by AI slop.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025-2026 quantitative data was found on "aura" memes specifically.
- No hard data was found on Gen Z attention-span metrics (most "8-second" claims are debunked or unsourced). Parasocial attachment to animated characters on Reels is unquantified in the sources found.

## 5. Benchmarks: share, comment, save and follow rates

### Takeaway
Brand benchmarks, which skew toward large accounts:
- Socialinsider (per follower): Reels share rate 0.10%, comment rate 0.06%, save rate 0.04%.
- Dash Social (my calculation, per reach): about 0.7% shares and 0.34% saves, from averages of 1.3K shares and 618 saves on 182K reach.
- Media & Entertainment Reels in Dash Social get about 0.8% shares per reach.

So a 0.1-0.3% share rate is at or above follower-based brand norms, but likely below what reach-based entertainment content earns. Whether it's low depends on the denominator (views vs reach vs followers).

### Cited Findings
- Socialinsider (21.3M posts, 405K brand accounts, organic only, Oct 2025-Mar 2026):
  - Reels share rate 0.10% of followers (carousels 0.08%, images 0.07%). Median 5 shares per Reel.
  - Reels comment rate 0.06% of followers. Median 33 comments.
  - Reels save rate 0.04%. Median 35 saves.
  - No breakdown by account size.
  - [Socialinsider, May 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram-engagement-report)
- Dash Social (3.3K+ brands, ≥1K followers, Jul-Dec 2025):
  - Overall average per Reel: 283K views, 182K reach, 1.3K shares, 618 saves.
  - Media & Entertainment: 545K views, 352K reach, 2.8K shares, 1K saves.
  - Engagement rate per view is 0.1-0.5% by industry (M&E 0.5%).
  - [Dash Social, Jun 2026](https://www.dashsocial.com/blog/instagram-reels-performance-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- Derived from Dash Social averages (my calculation): shares/reach is about 0.71% overall and about 0.80% for M&E. Saves/reach is about 0.34% overall. Shares/views is about 0.46% overall.
- A realistic goal for a story/entertainment account is a sends-per-reach rate of 0.5-1%+. Viral posts typically run far higher. This target is an inference, not a published threshold.
- Compare like with like. Instagram's insights show shares as a count. Divide by accounts reached, not by plays, to get "sends per reach" as Mosseri defines it.

### Gaps
- No credible public benchmark for follows per reach or profile-visit-to-follow conversion by account size.
- No TikTok-specific 2025-2026 share and comment rate benchmarks were retrieved in this pass.
- Benchmarks come from brand accounts, which may understate creator or entertainment norms.
